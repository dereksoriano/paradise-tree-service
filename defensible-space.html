Defensible space Paradise

Defensible space and fuel clearance in Paradise, CA

This page summarizes statewide and Town of Paradise rules as they appeared in the official sources cited below. It is general information, not legal advice, and it is not a substitute for an inspection by the Town or CAL FIRE.

Statewide rule: about 100 feet (PRC 4291)

California Public Resources Code section 4291 requires a person who owns, leases, controls, operates, or maintains a building or structure in the state responsibility area to maintain defensible space of 100 feet from each side and from the front and rear of the structure, but not beyond the property line, except where another state law, local ordinance, rule, or regulation requires a greater distance or work beyond the line (and then only with the adjacent owner’s written consent when work would occur on that property).

The same section says the intensity of fuels management may vary within that 100-foot perimeter, with more intense fuel reductions between 5 and 30 feet around the structure, and an ember-resistant zone required within 5 feet of the structure based on regulations from the State Board of Forestry and Fire Protection. Single, well-pruned trees or other vegetation that do not form a path for fire or embers can be treated differently than untreated fuel.

CAL FIRE’s public guidance describes a “lean, clean, and green” area within 30 feet and reduced fuel out to 100 feet or the property line, including mowing annual grass to a maximum height of 4 inches and pruning lower branches (CAL FIRE describes removing branches at least six feet from the ground). CAL FIRE also notes that some local agencies set stricter standards than the state minimum.

About the statewide 5-foot “Zone 0” ember-resistant zone.

PRC 4291 says the ember-resistant zone requirement does not take effect for new structures until the Board updates its regulations and guidance, and it takes effect for existing structures three years after the effective date for new structures. The Board of Forestry’s Zone 0 page described ongoing Office of Administrative Law review of that rule package (the page referenced a September 2026 guidance document and said staff would update the page when review is complete). Do not assume the statewide Zone 0 regulation is already in force without checking the Board or CAL FIRE. The Town of Paradise already has its own 5-foot noncombustible / no-vegetation rule, described below.

Town of Paradise rules (Municipal Code Chapter 8.58)

Paradise Municipal Code Chapter 8.58 is titled “Defensible Space and Hazardous Fuel Management.” The 2026-10 edition reviewed for this scaffold is marked updated 2026-10-04 and includes Ordinance No. 642 (adopted February 11, 2025, effective March 13, 2025) plus earlier amendments. Key requirements in section 8.58.060 include:

  • 0–5 feet at the structure: Only noncombustible material is allowed within five (5) feet of any building or structure. No vegetation may exist within or overhang within five (5) feet of the structure; overhanging limbs or branches shall be removed. Exterior walls must have a six (6) inch noncombustible vertical clearance from grade. Fencing within five (5) feet of a structure must be noncombustible.
  • Roofs, chimneys, and canopies: Keep roofs free of leaves, needles, or other combustible materials. Remove the portion of any tree that extends within ten (10) feet of a chimney or stovepipe outlet. Maintain trees adjacent to or overhanging a structure free of dead and dying wood. Tree placement should keep the mature canopy no closer than five (5) feet to the edge of the structure.
  • Driveways and streets: Property owners must establish and maintain a fuel break on both sides of any street or driveway. That fuel break extends horizontally to ten (10) feet and must reach a vertical clearance of fourteen (14) feet. The fire chief or designee may require a greater distance. This applies to public and private driveways and to public or private streets that border or bisect a property.
  • What a “fuel break” means in this chapter: Section 8.58.030 defines a fuel break in part as weeds, grass, and brush mowed and removed to a maximum height of four (4) inches; ladder fuels and dead portions of trees and shrubs removed; trees pruned six (6) feet from the ground (or one-third of overall height for shorter trees); and downed trees and slash removed.
  • Parcel-size rules: Parcels one and one-half (1½) acres or less must provide a fuel break for the entire parcel. Parcels greater than 1½ acres must maintain a fuel break a minimum of fifty (50) feet from the property line around the perimeter and one hundred (100) feet from any structure. Section 8.58.060(B) also says property owners shall not permit any accumulation of combustible materials within fifty (50) feet of the property line, with possible greater distance if needed to protect structures on an adjacent property.

The fire chief or designee may require additional fuel modification. The chapter also covers inspections, enforcement, and a certificate of compliance process before certain transfers of ownership. Read the current code for the full text, exceptions process, and any later amendments.

How this relates to tree work

Typical jobs connected to these rules include removing trees and limbs that overhang or sit within five feet of a structure, raising canopies, taking out dead or ladder fuels, clearing driveway clearance corridors, and reducing vegetation out toward 100 feet or across a small parcel. A landing-page contractor, once attached, would still need to follow license, insurance, and safety rules, and homeowners remain responsible for their own compliance.

FAQ

How much defensible space is required in Paradise?

California Public Resources Code section 4291 requires owners of structures in the state responsibility area to maintain defensible space of 100 feet from each side of the structure, but not beyond the property line unless another law or local ordinance requires it. The Town of Paradise also has its own rules in Municipal Code Chapter 8.58, including a 5-foot noncombustible zone at the structure, fuel-break rules for streets and driveways, and parcel-size rules (entire parcel if 1½ acres or less; at least 50 feet from the property line plus 100 feet from structures if larger).
